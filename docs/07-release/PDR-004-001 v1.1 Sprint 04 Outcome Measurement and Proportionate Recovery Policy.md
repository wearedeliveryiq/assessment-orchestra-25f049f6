> **Controlled-amendment notice.** This version preserves PDR-004-001 v1.0 Sections 2–8 and 10–13, including every S4-010 rule and golden fixture, without amendment. It replaces the former Tier 1/Tier 2/Tier 3 RPO/RTO and isolated-rehearsal policy with the current-stage recovery policy in Section 3 below. The replacement is an explicit founder risk-and-cost decision; it is not evidence that a restore occurred.

## 1. Decision Summary

The following decisions are approved:

1. All PDR-004-001 v1.0 outcome-measurement rules remain locked and unchanged.
2. The former Tier 1 RPO <=15 minutes, RTO <=4 hours, point-in-time recovery and isolated-restore rehearsal requirements are removed for the current product stage.
3. No fixed RPO or RTO is promised while DeliveryIQ relies on the documented current Lovable Cloud controls.
4. Daily database backups, roughly 14-day retention and in-place restore to an available snapshot are the approved current recovery basis.
5. Residual data loss, downtime, scope exclusions, schema reconciliation and unmeasured recovery duration are explicitly accepted.
6. Recovery evidence must remain truthful: no restore, recovery point or duration may be claimed unless actually observed.
7. PDR-004-002 v1.0 is superseded. A qualifying Lovable enterprise service or alternative Tier 1 architecture is no longer required for Sprint 04 acceptance.

Commercial rationale: the former recovery tier is disproportionate to the current product's data risk and maturity, and the Lovable cost required to pursue it is not justified at this stage. This decision must be revisited when the risk or commercial context changes materially.

## 3. Replacement Current-Stage Recovery Policy

This section replaces PDR-004-001 v1.0 Section 9 in full.

### 3.1 Approved available controls

DeliveryIQ may rely on the controls Lovable documents and actually exposes for the project:

- one database backup per day;
- retention of available backups for roughly 14 days;
- in-place restoration of database schema and data to a selected available snapshot;
- temporary database unavailability while restore runs;
- a logical database export, subject to documented size and frequency limits, as a portability control rather than a recovery-time guarantee; and
- separate download or reconstruction treatment for assets and components excluded from database backup/export.

The operational record must distinguish documented provider behaviour from project-specific observation. Provider documentation can establish the control design, but it cannot establish that a particular restore ran or how long it took.

### 3.2 No fixed recovery promise

DeliveryIQ makes no fixed RPO or RTO commitment at the current product stage. Specifically:

- arbitrary point-in-time recovery is not claimed;
- a recovery point no older than 15 minutes is not claimed;
- isolated or side-by-side self-service restore is not claimed;
- a four-hour recovery time is not claimed; and
- a logical export is not represented as a managed backup restore.

Any future customer, contractual, regulatory or product statement must use this truthful position unless a later locked decision is supported by demonstrated capability.

### 3.3 Minimum operational controls

The Head of Software owns recovery readiness; the Product Owner is accountable for residual-risk acceptance and customer commitments.

At minimum:

1. confirm and record that the project's backup control is visible before customer enablement under the accepted Sprint 04 release, after a material datastore/platform change and at least monthly while customer data is present;
2. retain a recovery runbook covering authorisation, snapshot selection, expected data-loss cutoff, maintenance/customer communication, in-place restore, application/schema reconciliation, security checks and controlled reopening;
3. restrict restore initiation to authorised project editors under an internally recorded decision; use separation of duties for planned restores where practical;
4. record the selected snapshot time, decision actor, invocation time, completion time and verification results whenever a real restore occurs;
5. verify migrations/schema compatibility, RLS, client-role denial, tenant isolation, immutable controls, audit continuity, idempotency and safe feature flags after any real restore;
6. maintain separate treatment for storage files, secrets, edge-function or scheduled-job configuration, authentication consequences and other items excluded from database backup/export;
7. use logical export only as an additional portability or pre-change safeguard within its documented limits, never as an unsupported RPO/RTO promise; and
8. do not perform a destructive in-place production restore solely to manufacture acceptance evidence.

### 3.4 Minimum Sprint 04 evidence

Sprint 04 recovery acceptance requires:

- an authoritative platform-capability assessment with source references;
- a project-specific read-only assessment that records what was and was not observed;
- a recovery runbook or controlled tabletop covering the steps in Section 3.3;
- named operational ownership and restore decision authority;
- explicit Product Owner/founder acceptance of residual risks;
- release controls that prevent unsupported recovery claims; and
- a statement that no restore pass is claimed when no restore occurred.

An actual restore rehearsal is not required for Sprint 04 acceptance under PB-004A. A real restore, when later performed for an authorised reason, must be fully evidenced and used to improve the runbook.

### 3.5 Recovery decision and execution

Before an in-place restore:

1. identify the incident or approved operational reason;
2. identify the latest suitable available snapshot and disclose the expected loss window;
3. record Product Owner/Head of Software authorisation, or the incident command decision for an emergency;
4. place affected service into controlled maintenance/fail-closed operation;
5. record the selected snapshot and invocation time;
6. restore through the documented platform control;
7. reconcile application revision, migrations, schema and governed configuration;
8. run the security, tenant, immutability, audit and feature checks in Section 3.3; and
9. reopen service only after recorded verification succeeds.

If verification fails, service remains fail closed and recovery/escalation continues. Immutable application records must not be edited to conceal recovery loss or reconciliation.

## 4. Accepted Residual Risks

Matt Prust explicitly accepts the following for the current product stage:

| Risk                               | Accepted current consequence                                                      | Required control                                                               |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Snapshot age                       | Database changes since the selected available backup may be lost                  | Disclose cutoff before restore; record actual selected snapshot                |
| In-place restore                   | Restore affects the project database rather than an isolated target               | Authorise carefully; use maintenance/fail-closed mode; verify before reopening |
| Service interruption               | Database is unavailable during restore and recovery duration is unmeasured        | Communicate incident/maintenance; record actual times when used                |
| Schema mismatch                    | Restored schema may not match the deployed application                            | Reconcile revision and migrations before reopening                             |
| Storage exclusion                  | Storage-bucket files are not included in database backup/export                   | Maintain separate asset treatment and do not imply coverage                    |
| Export limitations                 | Export is frequency/size limited and excludes platform components                 | Treat only as portability/pre-change evidence                                  |
| Authentication/configuration scope | Usable passwords, secrets and some runtime configuration are excluded from export | Maintain separate controlled reconstruction procedures                         |
| No isolated rehearsal              | A safe self-service side-by-side restore is not currently documented              | Do not claim rehearsal; revisit architecture when risk justifies it            |

## 5. Review Triggers

The Product Owner and Chief Solution Architect must review this policy when any of the following occurs:

- a customer contract or service level requires a recovery commitment;
- regulated or materially sensitive data is introduced;
- the volume, value or irreproducibility of customer data increases materially;
- a paying customer or design-partner risk assessment rejects the residual risk;
- Lovable changes its backup, restore, export or enterprise capability;
- a real recovery incident exposes unacceptable loss or downtime;
- the platform architecture or system-of-record location changes; or
- the cost/risk balance no longer supports the current decision.

A future fixed objective requires a versioned policy, demonstrated architecture and measured evidence before it is promised.
