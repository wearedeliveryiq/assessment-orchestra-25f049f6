## 2. Model retained

The approved five domains and fifteen capabilities remain:

1. **Direction & Value** — Strategic Alignment; Portfolio & Prioritisation; Benefits & Value Realisation.
2. **Leadership & Governance** — Sponsorship & Accountability; Governance & Decision-Making; Risk, Assurance & Resilience.
3. **Delivery System** — Delivery Approach & Lifecycle; Planning, Control & Dependencies; Capacity & Delivery Ecosystem.
4. **People & Enablement** — Leadership, Culture & Collaboration; Stakeholder Engagement & Change Adoption; Delivery Capability & Enablement.
5. **Insight & Adaptation** — Data, Reporting & Decision Insight; Learning, Adaptability & Improvement; Digital, Automation & Responsible AI.

Project and programme delivery remain supported contexts rather than separate capability slots. This is why four submitted project/programme-specific questions cannot be retained without weakening the approved cross-SME/enterprise model.

## 3. Question-role correction

The submitted catalogue identifies one most-discriminating Snapshot question per capability; it does not define Foundation, Practice and Evidence roles. v2.0 imposed those roles and rewrote the questions to fit them.

The approved v2.1 rule is:

| Question role | Capability weight | Customer use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Snapshot signal | 40% | Used in the free Snapshot and paid Overview |
| Supporting signal 1 | 30% | Used in the paid Overview |
| Supporting signal 2 | 30% | Used in the paid Overview |

This retains the existing 40/30/30 weighting while removing misleading internal labels. Capability availability remains at least two eligible answers and at least 60% eligible weight. Evidence recency and perspective breadth remain separately declared confidence metadata; they are not fabricated from a question label.

The v2.1 question IDs use `.snapshot`, `.supporting_1` and `.supporting_2`. A v2.0 response is never reinterpreted under a v2.1 ID.

## 5. Four minimal question edits

### 5.1 Q23 — remove project-only scope

**Submitted:** How standardised is the approach to running a project, regardless of who leads it?

**Approved:** How consistently is a shared delivery approach used and adapted, regardless of who leads the work?

**Reason:** Delivery DNA assesses organisational delivery across project, programme, portfolio, product and change contexts. The approved wording preserves the construct while removing project-only and overly rigid “standardised” language.

| Level | Submitted anchor | Approved anchor | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emerging | Approach varies entirely by individual — no shared method. | Approach varies entirely by individual — no shared method. | EXACT |
| Developing | Some common elements exist but are applied inconsistently. | Some common elements exist but are applied inconsistently. | EXACT |
| Established | A standard approach is generally used across projects. | A shared approach is generally used across delivery work. | **EDIT** |
| Leading | A consistent, well-adopted approach is used and adapted appropriately by context. | A consistent, well-adopted approach is used and adapted appropriately by context. | EXACT |

### 5.2 Q20 — remove programme/workstream-only scope

**Submitted:** How well are dependencies across workstreams identified and actively managed?

**Approved:** How well are dependencies across delivery work identified and actively managed?

**Reason:** Dependencies occur within and across projects, programmes, products, functions and suppliers. All four submitted anchors remain exact.

### 5.3 Q36 — include the approved capacity capability

**Submitted:** How scalable is our delivery support model as the volume or complexity of work grows?

**Approved:** How scalable is our delivery support and capacity model as the volume or complexity of work grows?

**Reason:** The current capability explicitly covers capacity as well as enablement and ecosystem support. All four submitted anchors remain exact because they refer neutrally to “the model”.

### 5.4 Q24 — remove project-only scope

**Submitted:** How well do project teams surface and resolve issues before they escalate?

**Approved:** How well do delivery teams surface and resolve issues before they escalate?

**Reason:** The behaviour applies across all approved delivery contexts. All four submitted anchors remain exact.

## 6. Four new questions and seventeen non-submitted anchors

These are the only wholly new questions. Together with the single edited Q23 Established anchor, they account for all seventeen non-submitted anchors.

### 6.1 Delivery Approach & Lifecycle — supporting signal

**Question:** How consistently do teams adapt their delivery approach using feedback, risk and current evidence?

- **Emerging** — Teams follow whatever approach they inherit or change it reactively, with little use of feedback, risk or evidence.
- **Developing** — Some teams adapt their approach, but changes are inconsistent or depend mainly on individual judgement.
- **Established** — Most teams adapt a shared approach using feedback, risk and current delivery evidence.
- **Leading** — Teams continuously refine proportionate delivery approaches using outcomes, learning, risk and current evidence.

**Reason:** The submitted catalogue covers consistency of ways of working but does not test whether the approach is deliberately adapted to context and evidence.

### 6.2 Capacity & Delivery Ecosystem — Snapshot

**Question:** How consistently are resources and internal or external commitments rebalanced as priorities and constraints change?

- **Emerging** — Commitments remain in place even when priorities change or capacity constraints become clear.
- **Developing** — Some rebalancing occurs, but usually late, inconsistently or through escalation.
- **Established** — Resources and commitments are generally rebalanced when priorities and material constraints change.
- **Leading** — Demand, capacity and internal or external commitments are continuously balanced through timely, transparent trade-offs.

**Reason:** The submitted catalogue does not contain a standalone question that tests active demand-capacity rebalancing across internal and external delivery contributors.

### 6.3 Capacity & Delivery Ecosystem — supporting signal

**Question:** How clearly can leaders see capacity, critical skills and supplier constraints before committing delivery work?

- **Emerging** — Commitments are made with little reliable view of available capacity, critical skills or supplier constraints.
- **Developing** — Some constraints are visible, but the view is incomplete, inconsistent or assembled after commitments are made.
- **Established** — Leaders usually have a current enough view of capacity, critical skills and supplier constraints before committing work.
- **Leading** — A trusted, forward-looking capacity and constraint view routinely shapes investment, sequencing and sourcing decisions.

**Reason:** The approved capability covers people, skills, suppliers and operational constraints; no submitted question covers this complete construct.

### 6.4 Leadership, Culture & Collaboration — Snapshot

**Question:** How consistently do leaders create clarity, invite constructive challenge and remove cross-team barriers?

- **Emerging** — Leaders provide inconsistent direction, challenge is discouraged or barriers persist between teams.
- **Developing** — Some leaders create clarity and invite challenge, but experience varies and cross-team barriers remain.
- **Established** — Most leaders create clear direction, constructive challenge and effective collaboration across teams.
- **Leading** — Leaders consistently create psychological safety, shared ownership and rapid collaboration across organisational boundaries.

**Reason:** The submitted catalogue covers sponsorship, stakeholder leadership and team improvement, but not the broader leadership and culture environment required by the approved capability.
