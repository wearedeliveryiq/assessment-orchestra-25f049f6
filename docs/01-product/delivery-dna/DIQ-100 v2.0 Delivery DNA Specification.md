## 1. Product decision in one page

Delivery DNA 2.0 will be a scale-neutral, method-neutral, evidence-led assessment of how an organisation—or a clearly defined part of it—directs, governs, delivers, enables and improves project-based change.

The recommended model is:

- five customer-facing domains;
- three capabilities in each domain;
- fifteen capabilities in total;
- three questions for each capability: Foundation, Practice and Evidence;
- 45 questions in the complete paid Delivery DNA Overview;
- one Practice question from every capability in the free fifteen-question Delivery DNA Snapshot;
- four behaviourally anchored response choices—Emerging, Developing, Established and Leading—plus Not applicable;
- five indicative domain levels in the Snapshot, with no numeric score;
- full domain, capability, confidence and priority intelligence in the paid Overview; and
- a clean replacement of Delivery DNA 1.0 with no response or result migration.

This corrects three weaknesses in the 1.0 model:

1. programme and project delivery are treated as separate organisational capabilities even where the assessed organisation does not use those labels;
2. modern delivery themes—adaptability, delivery capability, collaboration, digital enablement and responsible AI—do not have a coherent home; and
3. thirteen capability signals create a crowded Snapshot result without a simple executive story.

## 2. Product purpose and customer promise

### 2.1 Primary customer and market range

The primary respondent is a leader who is accountable for, sponsors or oversees project-based delivery, transformation or material business change. The respondent need not be a project-management specialist.

Delivery DNA must work without structural change for:

- an SME assessing its whole delivery system;
- a larger organisation assessing the enterprise as a whole;
- an enterprise assessing a business unit, division, function or defined delivery portfolio; and
- later multi-scope and multi-stakeholder Enterprise experiences built on the same versioned model.

SMEs remain an important initial route to market, but the taxonomy, questions, data model and result language must not assume a particular company size, formal PMO, central delivery function or delivery method.

### 2.2 Assessment scope — Founder approved

Every assessment is bound to one explicit organisational scope. Supported scope types are:

1. whole organisation;
2. business unit or division;
3. function; or
4. defined delivery portfolio or delivery system.

An individual project is not a Delivery DNA assessment scope because the model diagnoses organisational delivery capability rather than project health. A future project-specific diagnostic may use the shared engine under separate product rules.

The scope has a stable ID, type, customer-visible name and parent organisation. Before the questions begin, the journey states:

> For this assessment, “the organisation” means **{assessment scope name}**. Answer for how project and change delivery usually works across this scope today.

This allows natural question wording without making every prompt read like a data template.

A single completed assessment is explicitly a **lead perspective** supported by the evidence available to that respondent. It is not presented as independently verified truth for the enterprise or assessed scope.

An enterprise may run multiple separately scoped assessments. Aggregation, comparison, roll-up, weighting and cross-scope trend claims require the future Delivery DNA Perspectives or Enterprise authority; they are not inferred by averaging 2.0 results.

### 2.3 Customer promise

> Delivery DNA helps you see what enables delivery across your organisation, where avoidable friction is constraining outcomes, and what to address first.

Delivery DNA does not guarantee project success, independently certify maturity, benchmark the customer without an approved comparison population, or attribute organisational outcomes to one capability.

## 4. Capability definitions

### 4.1 Direction & Value

| Capability | Definition | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic Alignment | Connects delivery priorities and outcomes to organisational strategy and context. | Leaders use intended outcomes to make trade-offs and can see whether delivery contributes to strategy. |
| Portfolio & Prioritisation | Selects, sequences, starts, stops and rebalances work within realistic constraints. | Investment and capacity decisions use transparent criteria, current evidence and explicit trade-offs. |
| Benefits & Value Realisation | Defines, owns, measures and sustains the value expected from change. | Benefits have accountable owners and measures, remain visible after implementation and drive corrective action. |

### 4.2 Leadership & Governance

| Capability | Definition | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sponsorship & Accountability | Establishes active executive ownership for outcomes, decisions and barrier removal. | Sponsors remain engaged, make decisions and follow actions through rather than lending their name only. |
| Governance & Decision-Making | Provides proportionate decision rights, forums, escalation and accountability. | Decisions are timely, recorded, acted on and appropriate to the risk and complexity of the work. |
| Risk, Assurance & Resilience | Anticipates uncertainty, tests confidence and responds to threats, opportunities and disruption. | Material risks and assurance findings are current, owned, challenged and resolved or consciously accepted. |

### 4.3 Delivery System

| Capability | Definition | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery Approach & Lifecycle | Selects and tailors predictive, iterative, incremental or hybrid approaches to the work. | Teams use a clear lifecycle and adapt methods to value, uncertainty, complexity and feedback. |
| Planning, Control & Dependencies | Creates realistic plans, manages interdependencies and uses forecasts and change control to act early. | Plans remain current, dependencies are visible and variance leads to timely decisions and corrective action. |
| Capacity & Delivery Ecosystem | Aligns people, funding, skills, suppliers and operational constraints with delivery demand. | Capacity and constraints are visible, commitments are credible and internal and external contributors work to clear outcomes. |

### 4.4 People & Enablement

| Capability | Definition | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leadership, Culture & Collaboration | Creates clarity, constructive challenge, psychological safety and cross-boundary collaboration. | Leaders enable teams to surface problems early, make decisions and work across organisational boundaries. |
| Stakeholder Engagement & Change Adoption | Understands affected people, prepares change and sustains adoption. | Stakeholders shape the change, impacts are actively managed and evidence shows adoption and use. |
| Delivery Capability & Enablement | Builds delivery skills and provides proportionate standards, coaching, tools and PMO-style support. | Teams receive useful enablement matched to need, and support improves consistency and decision quality rather than adding ceremony. |

### 4.5 Insight & Adaptation

| Capability | Definition | What good looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data, Reporting & Decision Insight | Produces trusted, timely information that supports decisions and exposes uncertainty. | Reports explain outcomes, forecasts, risks and decisions rather than summarising activity alone. |
| Learning, Adaptability & Improvement | Uses feedback, outcomes and experience to adapt delivery and embed improvement. | Lessons and signals change future behaviour, methods and controls, with evidence that improvements stick. |
| Digital, Automation & Responsible AI | Applies digital tools, automation and AI where they improve delivery, with accountable human oversight and proportionate safeguards. | Use is outcome-led, integrated into work, permission-aware, reviewed by people and measured for value, quality and unintended effects. |

Non-use of AI is not automatically scored as poor performance. Questions assess whether relevant opportunities are selected and governed intentionally. **Not applicable** remains available where the combined digital, automation and AI capability genuinely does not apply, with a mandatory reason.

## 5. Question architecture

Every capability contains:

| Dimension | Weight in paid Overview | Tests |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Foundation | 30% | Whether purpose, ownership, roles, standards and intended outcomes are clear |
| Practice | 40% | What routinely happens across relevant delivery activity |
| Evidence | 30% | Whether recent, verifiable records, measures, decisions or outcomes support the response |

The exact locked wording and four answer anchors for every question are in DIQ-100A. Questions are:

- written for a business leader across SME and enterprise settings rather than a professional-method expert;
- observable, neutral, non-leading and answerable from current practice;
- method, sector, tool and vendor neutral;
- focused on consistency across the declared assessment scope rather than exceptional projects;
- one construct per question wherever practical;
- clear about evidence without requesting confidential material in the assessment; and
- suitable for en-GB and a reading age appropriate for a broad business audience.

## 6. Response model — Founder approved

Use the submitted four-choice behaviourally anchored scale. Customers select the statement that most closely reflects usual practice across the declared scope; they do not select a number:

| Internal value | Customer label | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Emerging | Practice is absent, reactive, informal, isolated or materially inconsistent. |
| 2 | Developing | Some relevant practice exists, but application, ownership or evidence remains uneven. |
| 3 | Established | The practice is defined and applied across most relevant delivery activity with useful supporting evidence. |
| 4 | Leading | The practice is embedded, outcome-led, reviewed and improved using current evidence. |

`not_applicable`, missing and controlled exclusion semantics remain fail-closed and non-contributing. Not applicable requires a customer reason and means the question genuinely does not apply; it must never be used to mean that the respondent does not know. A respondent may skip a question rather than estimate. A skipped response remains missing, never becomes zero and reduces coverage or confidence where applicable.

## 7. Delivery DNA Snapshot 2.0 — Founder approved

### 7.1 Journey

The Snapshot contains the fifteen Practice questions, one from each capability, in five clearly signposted groups. Existing save-first auto-advance, Back/edit, N/A reason, progress, accessibility and branded preparation behaviour remain.

The Snapshot should normally take six to eight minutes. It collects no PII before explicit registration.

### 7.2 Indicative result

The free result contains:

1. one overall **indicative delivery maturity** level;
2. five indicative domain levels;
3. a five-axis accessible domain-profile chart;
4. up to two positive signals;
5. up to two areas to explore;
6. the existing caveat and evidence boundary; and
7. one relevant, clearly separated industry-context statement with visible source footnote; and
8. a clear **Save my Snapshot** continuation.

It does not expose numeric scores, percentages, fifteen capability results, confidence, patterns, recommendations, a roadmap, benchmark comparisons or industry-relative claims. The experience must be a premium executive result rather than a survey-completion screen: branded preparation, a polished accessible five-axis spider chart, clear outcomes, concise narrative and an obvious continuation are mandatory.

### 7.3 Indicative calculation

- Use only answered 1–4 responses; N/A and missing responses are non-contributing.
- Each answered Practice question has equal weight.
- A domain level requires at least two of its three Practice questions.
- The overall level requires at least twelve of fifteen answered questions and at least two answered questions in every domain.
- Apply equal-width boundaries to the unrounded mean: Emerging `1.00–<1.75`; Developing `1.75–<2.50`; Established `2.50–<3.25`; Leading `3.25–4.00`.
- The five-axis profile plots the unrounded mean for each available domain but displays the level, not the number.
- No Snapshot calculation invokes or impersonates the full Delivery Intelligence Engine.

Mandatory caveat:

> This is an indicative view based on one practice signal from each Delivery DNA capability. It is not your complete Delivery DNA result and may change when foundations and supporting evidence are assessed.

### 7.4 Premium preparation and result hierarchy

After the fifteenth response is saved, show a truthful four-to-six-second preparation state headed **Building your Delivery DNA Snapshot** with supporting text **We’re reviewing the patterns across your 15 responses.** Progressively show:

1. Reviewing your delivery-practice responses
2. Building your five-domain profile
3. Identifying positive signals

5. Preparing your personalised Snapshot

Then show **Your Snapshot is ready** and reveal the result. Do not claim use of AI, benchmarking, external comparison, evidence validation or the full Delivery Intelligence Engine. Reduced-motion users receive equivalent static status changes.

The result hierarchy is:

1. branded completion hero;
2. **Your indicative delivery maturity** and non-numeric level;
3. mandatory caveat;
4. **Your indicative Delivery DNA profile** five-axis spider chart and accessible equivalent;
5. concise overall and five-domain interpretation;
6. up to two positive signals;
7. up to two areas to explore;
8. one separately labelled **Industry context** item where an approved mapping exists;
9. Saved Snapshot value panel and primary CTA; and
10. quiet tertiary **Start a new Snapshot** action.

The spider chart plots the unrounded domain means on five axes but exposes no numeric values. N/A-driven unavailable domains show a visible gap and **Unavailable**, never zero. The chart must remain understandable without colour; mobile may use numbered axes with an ordered legend.

## 9. Delivery DNA Overview 2.0 — Founder approved

The current configurable £295 one-off commercial position remains unchanged unless separately amended. The 2.0 customer completes the remaining thirty Foundation and Evidence questions after the fifteen Snapshot Practice responses are carried forward exactly.

The paid web result and board-ready report provide:

1. overall Delivery DNA score and maturity level;
2. five domain scores, levels and executive interpretations;
3. all fifteen capability scores, levels and evidence coverage;
4. Delivery Confidence Index, limitations and improvement prompts;
5. up to five strengths and five priority opportunities;
6. evidence-linked patterns and tensions across domains;
7. the top three prioritised recommendations with reason, impact, effort, first step and expected outcome;
8. a bounded 30/60/90-day direction;
9. relevant approved industry and professional-practice context, separated from the customer finding and carrying a visible source footnote;
10. plain-language explainability connecting conclusions to responses without exposing restricted logic; and
11. a downloadable report calculated from the same authorised projection as the web result.

Overview must feel like a decision product worth £295, not a longer questionnaire or a data dump.

### 9.1 Contextual evidence presentation — Founder approved

External evidence may explain why an independently derived finding matters but never determines the finding, score, confidence, band, priority or recommendation. The Snapshot may show one contextual item. The main Overview dashboard may show up to three. The report may place a relevant item alongside a priority opportunity where an approved mapping exists.

Each statement carries an asterisk, or a numbered note when multiple sources appear, with the source displayed visibly at the bottom of the page. The footnote includes publisher, publication title, evidence year and a short scope caveat. It must state that the evidence is general industry context rather than a benchmark or comparison with the assessed organisation. Evidence IDs and versions are retained with the generated projection for reproducibility.

### 9.2 Overview experience and report structure

The web result leads with decisions rather than data. Its hierarchy is overall position and confidence, five-domain profile, priority opportunities, strengths, cross-domain tensions, top three recommendations, 30/60/90-day direction, industry context, explainability and report download. Detailed capability results remain available without dominating the opening view.

The board-ready report uses seven purposeful sections generated from the same immutable projection:

1. **Executive position** — declared scope, overall score and level, confidence and executive summary
2. **Five-domain profile** — domain scores, accessible profile and concise interpretations
3. **Capability detail** — all fifteen capability scores, levels, coverage and evidence limitations
4. **What stands out** — strengths, priority opportunities and material cross-domain tensions
5. **What to do first** — top three recommendations, reasons, first steps, outcomes and success measures
6. **Direction for the next 90 days** — dependency-aware 30/60/90 direction and validation points
7. **Method, limitations and sources** — calculation boundary, lead-perspective caveat, traceability summary and complete industry-context footnotes

The primary result action is **Download my Delivery DNA Overview**. A contact, Assured, Action or Enterprise CTA is displayed only when that route is separately approved and genuinely available; otherwise it is omitted rather than replaced with a dead or misleading action.

## 10. Full scoring configuration — Locked

The locked configuration applies the approved four-choice scale and aggregation structure:

- eligible answers are integers 1–4 with status `answered`;
- normalised score is `(answer - 1) / 3 × 100`;
- Foundation/Practice/Evidence weights are `0.30/0.40/0.30`;
- a capability requires at least two eligible questions and eligible weight of at least `0.60`;
- a domain is the equal-weight mean of its available capabilities and requires at least two of three;
- overall score is the equal-weight mean of available capability scores;
- overall score requires at least eleven available capabilities and at least two available capabilities in every domain;
- calculations are stored to six decimal places and displayed half-up to one decimal place; and
- bands use unrounded values: Emerging `0–<25`; Developing `25–<50`; Established `50–<75`; Leading `75–100`.

The customer label **Fragile** is replaced by **Emerging** in 2.0. This does not require continued customer presentation of 1.0 bands after cutover.

DIQ-100B locks executable expectations for every exact boundary, N/A/missing behaviour, domain availability, overall admissibility and rounding.

### 10.1 Findings, patterns and recommendations

A capability is a **strength** when its unrounded score is at least `75` and capability confidence is at least `50`. A capability is a **priority opportunity** when its unrounded score is below `50` and capability confidence is at least `50`. Lower-confidence capability results are labelled insufficient evidence rather than forced into either classification. The Overview shows no more than five strengths and five priority opportunities; a threshold is never relaxed merely to fill the page.

DIQ-100A defines eleven deterministic cross-capability patterns: eight tension or risk patterns and three positive patterns. Every pattern has stable ID, version, prerequisites, minimum confidence, severity, conflict group and customer-safe explanation. Within an exclusive group, the highest priority pattern wins, followed by catalogue order and stable ID.

The recommendation catalogue contains one actionable route for each capability plus a low-confidence evidence route. The customer sees the top three eligible recommendations, ranked deterministically using impact `40%`, urgency `25%`, confidence `15%`, effort ease `10%` and dependency readiness `10%`. Recommendations carry a first step, expected outcome, success measures, prerequisites, exclusions and dependencies. Duplicate routes merge by deduplication group; no recommendation is invented by narrative generation.

The 30/60/90-day output is bounded direction, not a guaranteed transformation plan. It sequences eligible recommendations by dependencies and rank, with maximum capacities of three, three and four items. Cycles fail closed and unavailable items remain unscheduled with a safe reason.

## 11. Confidence and multi-perspective boundary

The 2.0 single-respondent Overview remains a lead perspective. Confidence measures evidential support, not whether the respondent is right.

Confidence uses five independent factors: required completion `35%`, capability coverage `25%`, response consistency `20%`, evidence recency `10%` and perspective breadth `10%`. Coverage uses fifteen capabilities. Evidence recency and perspective breadth are collected through two short, non-PII declarations at Overview completion. Unknown metadata contributes zero rather than being inferred.

Confidence bands are Low `0–<50`, Moderate `50–<75` and High `75–100`, using unrounded values. Capability confidence separately combines eligible question coverage `50%`, within-capability consistency `30%`, evidence recency `10%` and perspective breadth `10%`. Confidence describes evidential support, not correctness, independent verification or certainty.

Delivery DNA Perspectives must not average named respondents silently into this result. It requires separately approved aggregation, cohort, anonymity, disagreement, suppression and disclosure rules.

## 13. Research alignment and exclusions

The model is designed to remain method-neutral while covering current professional themes:

- value, adaptability, accountability, PMO and procurement in PMI's PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition;
- systems thinking, sustainability, leadership, data and AI in APM Body of Knowledge Eighth Edition;
- governance, portfolio management, planning/control, benefits, capacity, reporting, risk, change and solution delivery in GovS 002;
- contextual tailoring across predictive, adaptive and hybrid delivery; and
- human accountability, data quality, risk, safeguards and value-led use in PMI's 2026 AI standard.

Primary references:

- https://www.pmi.org/standards/pmbok
- https://www.apm.org.uk/apm-body-of-knowledge/
- https://projectdelivery.gov.uk/govs-002-project-delivery-functional-standard/
- https://www.iso.org/standard/74947.html
- https://www.pmi.org/standards/artificial-intelligence

These sources inform coverage; they do not supply DeliveryIQ scores, benchmarks or causal claims.

The core questionnaire intentionally does not create separate capabilities for sustainability, procurement, finance, quality, systems thinking or regulatory compliance. Their relevant principles are incorporated across strategy, value, risk, delivery approach, ecosystem, insight and responsible AI without making the product sector-specific or excessively long.
